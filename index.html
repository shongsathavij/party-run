                  How it works

                  What does Cakewalk do?
                  1. Tell it the party. The theme, the date, how many kids, your budget, and where it is.
                  2. Review the cart. It finds everything at real stores. Swap anything, change amounts, or ask for a change.
                  3. Approve and it shops. Store by store, with a pass for each. It stops and asks you before big buys.
                  How does it pay without my card?

                  When you approve the cart, your card is saved with Stripe. Then, for each store, Cakewalk makes a one-time pass: that store only, up to its amount, for 30 minutes. The store charges the pass. Neither the agent nor the store ever sees your card.

                  Can it spend more than I approve?
                  • Never your card. Stores get a pass, not your card number.
                  • Locked to one store. One store, one amount, 30 minutes.
                  • Your limit, enforced by Stripe. A charge over the pass is declined by the payment itself, not by the agent.
                  • What you see is what you pay. You approve the exact cart, and you get a signed receipt.

                  We tested it. A store asked for $169.99 on a $149.99 pass, and Stripe refused: “The requested amount is greater than the remaining amount capturable with this shared payment granted token.”

                  What do stores see?

                  Only what they need to fill the order: the items, the delivery ZIP, and a pass for their amount. In this demo nothing is sent to any store; Stripe runs in test mode.

                  Is this real?

                  The stores and listings are real, saved as a snapshot, and every item links to its page. The payments are real Stripe calls in test mode, so nothing is ordered and no money moves.
