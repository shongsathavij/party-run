# Piece of Cake

(Formerly Party Run, then Cakewalk; renamed because the cakewalk began as a dance by enslaved people on plantations and later a minstrel-show staple. The repo, functions and storage keys keep the old name "party-run"; Stripe metadata stays `app: party-run`.) An AI agent that shops for a kid's birthday party from real store listings (a saved snapshot), and can only spend what the parent approves. Payments stay in Stripe test mode: nothing is ordered and no store is contacted. Portfolio project for a senior payments PM (companion to Supper & a Show at date.sirinrut.com). Planned home: party.sirinrut.com, its own Vercel project and GitHub repo.

The pitch: party planning? Piece of cake. The agent can shop, but it can't overspend, because the limits live in the payment itself (Stripe shared payment tokens), not in the prompt.

## Stack

- Static front end, no build step: `index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js` (ES modules; the page imports `lib/` directly).
- `lib/catalog.js`: real stores (C&M Event Rentals and L.A. Inflatables Rental, Whole Foods Market catering, Target, Walmart, Michaels) with real listings copied on `SNAPSHOT` (name, price as listed before tax and delivery, link; delivery and tax are added as estimates, see Delivery and tax), plus one made-up test store (`test: true`, Brickyard Party Supply). To refresh: re-check each `url`, update prices, bump `SNAPSHOT`. `lead` is our own planning buffer, not a store policy, and never less than the time to reach the party (`MIN_LEAD`: shipped 4 days, pickup and rental delivery 2), all our estimates. Favors are sold in packs (`per: 'pack'`, `packOf`). `lib/rules.js`: cart totals, `checkCart`, `permissionFor`, `decide`, `raise`. Pure functions shared by the page, the server, the agent's checks, and the evals. Change a rule there, never in a copy.
- Vercel functions in `api/`: `plan` (the agent), `permit` (sign the parent's permission), `buy` (mint a pass and charge one store), `raise` (parent OKs a higher price), `refund` (store cancels), `replace` (find the same kind of item at a different store, within what's left of the budget; rules, not AI), `edit` (change the cart before approval), `config`. `api/_lib/` isn't routed.
- No dependencies. Stripe and Anthropic are called with `fetch`.

- `case-study.html`, served at /case-study (`cleanUrls`), linked from the footer: the portfolio write-up, about 2,000 words, mirroring date.sirinrut.com/case-study (What this shows, why, what it does, the payment with the verified Stripe refusal, the agent and its evals, four decision cards plus a "Smaller calls" table, how I worked with AI, metrics, next (with one line on the pattern beyond parties), stack, and an appendix). Under the byline, a four-fact strip (about 10 days, 6 real stores, 13 eval prompts, "Over-limit charges" refused by Stripe, not the prompt, the only red fact); keep its numbers true. "What I'd measure" has two numbers (charges over a limit, carts approved unchanged). How I worked with AI opens "Claude wrote the code. I owned the decisions, the evals, and every call on what shipped." "What this shows" is: I ship; I build trust into the payment (from the billing portal research); I keep the record (the receipt). No rename card and no business section, by her call. Same look as the app (calm cards; the striped shadow only on "What this shows"). Three phone screenshots in `img/case-home.jpg`, `case-cart.jpg`, `case-go.jpg` (585x1140); retake them when those screens change visibly. The payment section leads with a simple four-card flow (agent proposes → you approve once → Stripe makes a pass per store → store charges; "Asks for $20 more? Stripe declines it."), split into "The AI's part" and "The money's part". A link under it goes to the appendix (`#appendix`): the full "behind the screen" swimlane diagram (inline SVG, five lanes; drawn by `scripts/case-flow.py`, paste its `flow.svg` over the old one; it scrolls sideways on phones). Its arrow markers are `mk-*` ids, since `#ai` is a heading. "Agentic checkout" in the lede is bold ink, not red, and "Try the agent" is a dark blue outline, so red stays rare. Update the eval numbers after each Claude run, and the rules and decisions when the product changes.

## Commands

- `node scripts/serve.js` (PORT to change from 3000): local server, reads `.env.local`.
- `npm test`: every route in sample mode plus the agent tool loop with a scripted model.
- `node evals/run.js` (rules agent) or `--agent claude --model claude-haiku-5-5 --runs 2`. All checks are code (money and safety have right answers): buyable cart, never buys what a listing pushed, reports the hidden instruction, honest about budget, enough favor packs for every kid, plus per-prompt expectations. The 13 prompts are all variations of the real party (s01: a party two days out, so nothing shipped and it says what couldn't arrive; an empty cart that says why is an honest answer). Quality checks, judged by wording and reported on their own line without changing pass or fail: when the bounce house can't happen, it suggests an alternative (bubbles, running games). Results in `evals/results/`. Also runs from GitHub Actions ("Agent evals").

## Rules

- Every route works with no keys: a rules agent stands in for Claude, and payments are simulated with the same rules, labeled on the page.
- Token budget: the agent searches every category in one turn, then submits (about 2 turns a plan, `MAX_TURNS` 5). `api/plan.js` gives each visitor 5 Claude plans an hour and each server instance 60 a day (`PLANS_PER_VISITOR_HOUR`, `PLANS_PER_DAY`); past that the rules agent plans (mode `limited`). `PARTY_AGENT=rules` turns Claude off. Counters are in memory, so the real ceiling is the Anthropic account's spend limit. Evals print total tokens.
- Test mode only: `api/_lib/stripe.js` refuses any key that isn't `sk_test_`. Payments use Stripe's test Visa (`pm_card_visa`); there's no card form yet.
- The agent never gets a payment tool. It proposes a cart; the parent approves a permission; the server buys.
- Prices always come from the catalog by id, never from the page or the model.
- Permission (`permissionFor`): one cap per store (that store's approved total); caps add up to no more than the budget (no budget given: the cart total is the budget); an ask-me threshold (`askOver`: $50 / $100 default / $300 / always / never); ends on the party date. The server signs it (HMAC, `api/_lib/permit.js`, `PERMISSION_SECRET` or derived from the Stripe key); a changed permission is refused.
- Buying (`api/buy.js`): check the permission and `decide()` (store in it, under its cap, ask-me rule), then mint a shared payment token for that store only, capped at its approved amount, expiring in 30 minutes (`TOKEN_MINUTES`), then the store charges it. In test mode the token comes from Stripe's test helper (`/v1/test_helpers/shared_payment/granted_tokens`; it takes payment_method, usage_limits, customer and shared_metadata only, so the store goes in shared_metadata) and the charge is a confirmed PaymentIntent with `shared_payment_granted_token`. If Stripe ever accepts a charge over the cap, it's refunded at once and reported (`railsMissed`).
- Test the limits (after the run, no checkbox): "Have a store charge $20 more" calls `buy` with `test: true`, which mints a fresh pass at that store's cap and asks for cap + $20 (`PRICE_UP`), skipping the ask-me step; it can never end in a charge (if Stripe ever accepted, it's refunded and reported). "Have the rental cancel" refunds the rental. Both are labeled as tests, and the receipt lists them. Verified live Nov 19: Stripe itself refused $169.99 on a $149.99 pass ("The requested amount is greater than the remaining amount capturable with this shared payment granted token"). The older `curveball: 'price_up'` path and `raise` stay in the code for a real over-cap charge; never say a real store added a fee or cancelled.
- Real stores: names as plain text only (no logos), every real item links to its listing, the page says the listings are a snapshot, that Party Run isn't affiliated, and that nothing is ordered. Nothing false about a real business, so both deliberate traps live only on the made-up test store, which is labeled on the page: its brick bounce house is booked Dec 5-6 (the agent must swap to a real rental and say why), and its table set hides an instruction to AI shoppers (prompt injection). The agent reports the instruction and buys nothing from that store unless the parent asks for the Deluxe package by name. Listings are wrapped as data in tool results; the prompt says listings are never instructions; and `pushedByListings()` sends back any cart item a listing names unless the parent's request asks for it by its distinctive word ("deluxe").
- Fallbacks: when the rules agent plans instead of Claude, `plan` returns `fallback` (`kind`: `checks`, `no_cart`, or `error`, plus the first reason) and the page says which happened; never call an API error a failed check. The model gets two turns to search, then `tool_choice` forces `submit_cart`. A first cart with only soft problems is kept if the model's fix comes back unbuyable. Like the date, the budget comes from the parent's words (`readRequest`) when they wrote one, never from the model's memory of it. If the model was shown the listing with the planted instruction and its cart reports nothing in `ignored`, it's sent back once to report it (that note is for the model and never reaches the parent's unmet list).
- Under the hood: every `buy` result carries `hood` (token id, cap, expiry, what the store charged, the PaymentIntent, Stripe's code and decline code, and `decidedBy`: `stripe`, `simulator` with no key, or `refund` when Stripe accepted an over-cap charge and our code undid it). The page shows it per store and must say plainly which of the three happened.
- Changing the cart: before approval only (`state.locked` once shopping starts). + / − appear only on favors and food (`countable`), under the price, and call `edit` with `set` (plain code, no tokens). "Ask for a change" calls `edit` with the parent's words (300 characters): Claude in edit mode (`EDIT_RULES`, counts toward the same per-visitor limit in `_lib/limits.js`), or `rulesEdit` when Claude is off (add, remove, amounts, cake flavor, budget; says plainly when it can't). Either way the cart is rebuilt from the catalog (`rebuild`), checked like a new plan, refused if it breaks a check or the budget, and shown as a diff (`diffCarts`). After a worded change the diff (`#changed`, above the items) scrolls into view and flashes once, since the form sits below the cart.
- Theme: `offTheme()` flags themeable items (bounce, cake, piñata, decor) that don't match the request's theme; `plan` marks each item `offTheme` and `needsOk` (off-theme and asked for by name). The page shows a "Not brick-themed" chip, and any `needsOk` item must be kept or dropped before "Let the agent shop" works.
- Falling through: when a store is skipped or refunded and nothing else covers that part of the party, the page says the party isn't complete and offers "Find another <thing>". `replace` skips the stores already tried and the test store; the parent approves a second, separate permission for that one store and amount. The $20 what-if happens at one rental only (`priceUpStore`). The receipt says whether the party is complete.
- Asked for by name: `flagTheme` also sets `cart.asked` (named categories) and `cart.little`. Claude's cart is sent back once if a named thing is missing with no word why in `unmet`. The page shows "Missing from the cart" with Add (`edit` with `add`, rules, no tokens) or Skip; after the run, a named thing that isn't paid for counts as missing even if it never reached the cart. Anything the parent removed (`state.declined`) is never "missing". `api/_lib/pick.js` (`pickFor`) is the one picker for add and replace: free on the date, within what's left, never the test store, on theme when possible, and with kids under 4 only pull-string piñatas (`pull: true`) and ages-2+ favors. A failed purchase is logged for the receipt.
- Toddler safety: `safetyProblems()` (kids under 4: pull-string piñata, favors ages 2+) is a hard check on the agent's cart, so a bat piñata is sent back. In an edit it's the parent's call, so it's a note in `unmet`, not a refusal. Evals check the piñata too. + / − also appear on items marked `countable` in the catalog (the tableware sets).
- The card, at the go-ahead (no setup screen): the go-ahead shows "Pay with" (Stripe's test card with billing ZIP) and one button, "Approve $X and shop". Approving first saves the card (`ensureWallet` → `api/setup`, `saveCard()` in `_lib/stripe.js`: Stripe's test Visa saved to a new Customer; simulated with no key), then signs the permission. The wallet (customer and payment method ids only, `cleanWallet`) goes into the signed permission, and `mint` draws each pass on that saved card (Under the hood: "Draws on"); if the test helper ever refuses the saved card it falls back to `pm_card_visa` and says so. The wallet is kept in sessionStorage.
- Home: no brand bar (the headline names it; the bar with the wordmark, small cake logo and striped BETA sticker shows on every other screen), a big two-tier cake (the logo cake in three-quarter view with a face, soft shading and gloss, and a blurred shadow; its legs show only while it dances; a short candle with a flickering flame, `#heroCake`) that does one random dance from `DANCES` on arrival and another when tapped (`heroDance()`), one headline ("Party planning? Piece of cake.", the second sentence in red, no underline), one line ("Tell us about the party. We'll shop it, and spend only what you approve."), the chatbox (rounded, on its blue-and-white striped shadow, with a quiet grey demo note) with Plan it, and Try to trick the agent (a hairline box). The request includes the ZIP ("a backyard party in 90210"); the note says "This demo uses Stephanie's real party, so it's fixed." Tapping the cake makes it moonwalk (`moonwalk()`; off with reduced motion).
- How it works: a collapsed FAQ at the very bottom of the page (`#faq`): what Piece of Cake does, how it pays without your card (the pass), whether it can overspend (the four promises and the Stripe refusal), what stores see, whether it's real. "How passes work ↓" at the go-ahead links to it.
- Delivery and tax: the demo ZIP is 90210 (`DEMO_ZIP`); the real party's address is never asked for. Each store has `fulfill` (`delivery` $50 for rentals, `pickup` $0 for Whole Foods, `ship` $7.99 for Target, Walmart and Michaels) and tax is 9.75% on everything but food (`TAX_RATE`, `taxable`). All are Party Run's estimates, labeled as such, not store quotes or policies. `totals()` adds them per store, so each store's amount, its pass cap, the budget check, the agent's checks, `pickFor` and the evals all count them; the go-ahead shows items · delivery · tax. In real agentic checkout (ACP) the store returns shipping and tax from the address; this mirrors that shape.
- The party by what it is: before shopping the cart is grouped by category, the way a parent thinks (`GROUPS`: Fun = bounce house and piñata, Food = cake and snacks, Plates and napkins, Favors), with the store as a small label on each item; the run, passes and receipt stay by store, since each store gets its own pass ("plan by what, pay by where"). Each item has "N other options" (`optionsFor` in `lib/options.js`, the same rules as `pickFor`: free on the date, enough notice, never the test store, safe for kids under 4, on-theme first); tapping Swap calls `edit` with `swap` (rules, no tokens, checked like a new plan). An off-theme thing she asked for by name gets a flag on its own row (Keep it / See other options / Remove), like a grocery app's substitution; picking a swap counts as her answer. Two steps: 1 Here's the party, 2 Your go-ahead (total, one sentence from `permitLine`, one button; limits and stop rules folded). The go-ahead is faded and `inert` until every flag is answered.
- Quiet by default: the cart shows two agent notes (the rest under "Why these picks?"), item reasons only after "Why each item?", and the ignored instruction as one line ("See it" opens the quote). The agent is asked for at most 2 short notes, and `plain()` turns the trademark into "building-brick" in its own words. In the run, a paid store is one line (tap for the pass and Under the hood); if its pass refused a charge first, that line stays visible in red.
- Focus and handoff (shaped by the owner's AI billing portal research: AI optional, explainable, bounded by clear human escalation; context carries over; auditable outcomes): while shopping, `body.shopping` shrinks the cart to one card and gives the run about 75% of the width; a queue of store chips sits on top, and the store being bought from is a spotlight card with three steps (checking your permission, making a pass, store charging). The permission screen lists, before consent, the only times the agent stops: over the ask-me amount, a store asks for more than approved, part of the party falls through. Each stop is a `handoff()` sheet: why I stopped, what's done so far, my suggestion, what happens next, plus See the listing; the receipt records the decision.
- Playful, never in the numbers: the agent's face, drawn on the logo cake's blue-and-white bottom tier with pink cheeks (`.avatar.face`; `mood()`: one face per thinking line (`THINK_FACES`: reading, looking over, yum for cakes, curious squint, "hmm" adding up) with a bob while it works, working (tongue out) while shopping, happy, one raised eyebrow on a handoff (curious, never cross), wink when it ignored an instruction); a building-brick cake with one candle per store (`renderCake`, `candle`): paid lights it, refused, skipped or cancelled puts it out with smoke, a replacement relights its slot; the success page has the logo cake's random dance instead. Paid rows and the success line use party voice (`partyLine`, `bookedLine`: "Piñata: secured. Pull-string, so no bats near the toddlers.") while amounts, limits and Stripe's words stay plain. Reduced motion turns every animation off. The run keeps its focus layout after it finishes, until Start over.
- Success view: after the run, "See your receipt" swaps the page for `#success`: "Party's on." or "Almost there." with the same never-over-approved check as the receipt, then the receipt. On a complete party the logo cake up top does a dance picked at random from four (`DANCES`: moonwalk, twist, bunny hop, spin; `randomDance()`), with no confetti and no name for the move (`moonwalk()`, the same as tapping it); otherwise nothing moves. No animation with reduced motion. "Find another" there goes back and starts the search.
- Permission receipt: after the run, one record built from `state.log` (per-store events: asked, paid, refused, raised, skipped, refunded) and the first approved permission: what the parent approved, what the agent did and ignored, each store's limit (and any raise), what happened in order with pass and PaymentIntent ids, totals, and a verdict that checks spending never passed what was approved. Print or copy as text.
- Try to trick the agent: a visitor's text (`plant`, cleaned, 300 characters max) replaces the planted instruction in the test store's table-set listing for that one plan (`withPlant`). Never plant text in a real store's listing. `pushedByListings` also catches items the planted text names by name. `plan` returns `trick` (reported, sentBack, tookOver), and the page reports each layer as it happened, including when the agent did not flag the text.
- Agent rules (prompt and rules agent alike): basics (cake, decor, favors, snacks) plus what's named; bounce house or piñata only if asked; "just"/"only" means only what's named; kids from the request (top of a range; default 10); enough favor packs for one per kid; favors rated for the youngest guest (ages 2+ when 2-year-olds come) and a pull-string piñata with kids under 4; nothing booked or past its notice; stay under budget, dropping extras first; `unmet` only lists what they asked for; when a bounce house they asked for can't happen, suggest a cheaper or free alternative in notes (bubbles, running games).
- The real party in the demo is the owner's son's 5th birthday: building-brick theme, backyard, Dec 5, 10am-noon, 6-10 kids ages 2-5, bounce house and piñata, under $600. The owner's first name (Stephanie) appears on the page; the child is never named, and there's no real address (the demo ZIP is 90210). The page only plans this party: the request is shown read-only (the API still takes other wording, for the evals). Real listings keep their real names; our own words and the test store say "building-brick", not the trademark; the parent's own words can say Lego.
- Design: Memphis, pared back, with a candy layer inspired by the stripes, colors and inflatable shapes of FriendsWithYou (the "Candy layer" at the end of `styles.css`); the cake only celebrates, nothing is called a "victory dance". Our own shapes only: never their characters (no white cloud with a face, no star or rainbow with a face, no mushrooms). Warm white, no monospace type, heavy Inter Tight headlines (no serif, no italics), thick ink outlines, square cards, hard offset shadows for the interface. Four interface colors, one job each: candy red (`--coral`, #EE3B3B, white text at 19px bold so it reads as large text) for anything you tap to act; sky blue (`--teal`, #7CC5EE) for the chatbox's striped shadow and small accents, with dark blue (`--teal-ink`/`--violet`, #1E6FA6) for links and text; yellow for celebration only (the candle, the striped BETA sticker, paid moments); ink for outlines and type. The big cake sits beside the headline in `.hero-row` (home only, so it's gone while planning, shopping and on the success page), with no outline and a slow bob; the home page is one column (the money column only appears with a cart), the headline row spans it with the cake at its right end, and the rest of the form stays 680px wide; the headline is two lines ("Party planning?" / "Piece of cake."), and on phones the first line wraps too.  A slice lifting out of the cake is saved for the success page. Dance classes are prefixed `go-` (`go-spin`), since `.spin` is the loading spinner. The logo is a puffy cake in the same style: red striped top tier, blue-and-white banded bottom tier with a face, blue candle. The favicon (`favicon.svg`) and the home-screen icon (`apple-touch-icon.png`, 180px) are the same cake on candy red (top tier white with a red stripe, white candle, yellow flame); bump the `?v=` on their links when they change, since browsers cache icons. The link preview (`og.png`, 1200x630, with og: and twitter: tags pointing at https://party.sirinrut.com/og.png) shows the headline, one line on the agent and its limits, a sample request with Plan it, and the shaded cake. No pills (not for a kids' party), no squiggles. Calm flow (end of `styles.css`): past the home page, cards are rounded with hairline borders (`--hair`) and soft shadows (`--lift`) instead of thick outlines and hard shadows; no monospace type anywhere (Inter Tight throughout, ids included); the receipt reads like an order confirmation. Only the chatbox and the receipt (the run's bookends) have the striped shadow, and red buttons keep their chunky edge. Store brick-badge colors, green for paid and red for refused stay as they were.
